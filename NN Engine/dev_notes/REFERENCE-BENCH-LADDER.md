## ☠️ HOW TO READ THIS TABLE — the nodes column is not decoration

**At a fixed nominal DEPTH the engines do wildly different amounts of work.** Measured here: SF11 reaches
depth 10 on sts300 in **21k nodes per position** and SF19 in **11k**, while we need about **249k** — and
SF1.1 (2008) needs **315k**, i.e. MORE than us. Nominal depth is not a unit of work and is not comparable
across engines; it is a property of each engine's pruning and extension schedule.
★ That SF1.1 row is what makes the point unarguable: the spread is not "old vs new", it is "prunes little
vs prunes hard", and we sit at the un-pruned end with the 2008 engine.

⇒ **A depth-10 score column flatters whoever prunes least, which is us.** The long-quoted claim that we
"beat SF at fixed d10 on WAC" is therefore mostly a statement about tree size: at d10 SF18 solved 203/300
using ~4,751 nodes/position while we solved 262/300 using ~870k — roughly **183× the width** (2026-06-15).
That is not evidence our eval is better.

**The equal-NODES regime is the honest reading, and it is much harsher on us.** At our own ~249k budget
SF11 reaches **depth 15**, and its STS goes from 1983 (66.1%) to **2374 (79.1%)** while ours stays at 1796.
The gap widens from −187 to −578.

⚠️ Related standing cautions: `[[wac-at-d10-does-not-discriminate-strength]]` (WAC d10 is a fingerprint and
a tactical veto, never a score — and the suite has a ~20-position answer-disagreement floor, since SF at
d20 still only reaches 280/300) · `[[ebf-metric-is-not-comparable]]` · `[[equal-depth-ladder-is-biased-by-pruning]]`.

## ★★★★ 2026-09-21 — THE DECOMPOSITION: HOW MUCH OF THE GAP IS EVAL vs SEARCH

### The equal-nodes STS ladder is MONOTONE in engine strength — so it is the trustworthy instrument
| engine | STS @ 249,014 nodes | vs v2 |
|---|---|---|
| **v2 shipped** | **1854** | — |
| **SF1.1 (2008)** | **1991** (66.4%, 270k n/pos actual) | **+137** |
| SF11 | 2374 | +520 |
| SF15.1 classical | 2492 | +638 |
| SF15.1 NNUE | 2599 | +745 |
| SF18 | 2605 | +751 |
| SF19 | 2636 | +782 |

★★ Strictly increasing SF1.1 -> SF19, matching the known strength order of six engine generations. Contrast
WAC and fixed-depth STS, both of which are contaminated by tree size (SF1.1 outscores SF11 at d10 on 15x
the nodes). ⇒ **equal-nodes STS is the best cross-engine eval/strength instrument we own. Use it.**

### THE SPLIT — two independent instruments converge
**Instrument A: SF1.1 at equal nodes = +137.** SF1.1 is a 2008 HCE whose TERM SET is essentially ours
(material, PST, pawn structure, passers, mobility, king safety, space) and whose search is as unselective
as ours -- it needs 270k nodes to reach roughly the depth we reach in 249k. So with search held at
approximately our own level of sophistication, its hand-tuned CONSTANTS are worth about **+137 STS**.

**Instrument B: the SF11-eval oracle = +104 to +174.** `sf-oracle-eval-substitution` runs OUR search with
SF11's static eval: +174 STS at d6, +104 at d8. Same quantity from the other direction -- eval swapped,
search fixed.

⇒ **EVAL is worth roughly +110 to +175 STS points. The remaining ~350-420 of SF11's +520 is SEARCH.**
i.e. approximately **25-35% eval / 65-75% search**, and the eval portion is CALIBRATION, not coverage.

### ☠️ "MISSING EVAL TERMS" IS RULED OUT, THREE INDEPENDENT WAYS
1. **SF1.1 carries no term we lack** and beats us by +137 at equal nodes. No absent mechanism explains that.
2. **Thirteen consecutive move-null concepts** (2026-09), including the gap audit's top-ranked 4/4-universal
   MECHANISM (the passer path-safety ladder, rejected on 7 arms / 3 channels).
3. The naive fit toward SF11 scores **net -17 / -19 / -10** by breaking the cell where we already beat it.
⇒ The gap-audit premise -- enumerate terms the references have that we do not -- is **empirically dead**.
The eval lever is the **JOINT RETUNE**, which has never been run.

### WHERE THE SEARCH DEFICIT LIVES (already measured, not new)
- **Nodes to depth 10: ours 249,014 vs SF11's 21,000-26,265** (~10x). At our budget SF11 reaches **d15**.
- **EBF venue-matched: ours 1.914 vs SF11's 1.452** ⇒ our tree is broader per ply; we spend nodes on worse
  moves. That is MOVE ORDERING and pruning shape, not speed.
- **Profile: eval 11.5% of node cost, movegen 41.1%, ~42% unprofiled alpha-beta control flow.** ⇒ zeroing
  the eval entirely buys 1.13x. ☠️ Never justify an eval change on NPS.
- `MG_ISSAFE` is 2.08x `MG_PSEUDO` ⇒ **pin-aware legal move generation** is the indicated movegen fix.

⚠️ CAVEATS ON THE SPLIT. The oracle numbers are d6/d8, not equal-nodes, so the eval share is an estimate,
not a measurement. Attributing all of SF1.1's +137 to eval assumes its search ~= ours in selectivity, which
is approximate (it reaches slightly less depth at our budget, which if anything UNDERstates its eval edge).
Both instruments agree to within their error, which is the reason to believe the range at all.

## ★★★★ 2026-09-22 — SF1.1 SUBSYSTEM ABLATION: WHICH TERMS CARRY A HAND-TUNED HCE?

**Why this is possible at all:** SF1.1 exposes its eval subsystem weights as UCI spins (0-200, default
100) -- `Mobility (Middle Game)/(Endgame)`, `Pawn Structure (...)`, `Passed Pawns (...)`, plus
`King Safety Coefficient` (default 40, min 1). Setting a pair to 0 turns that subsystem OFF **in a
hand-tuned reference eval whose TERM SET matches ours**, with no recompile and no source edit. No other
local reference exposes this.

**Method:** `diagnostics/sf_ceiling_win.py ... UCI="Name=Value|Name=Value"`, sts300 @ d10, 1 thread,
128 MB, Windows python against the shipped 2008 `.exe`. Baseline **2104**.

| arm | STS | Δ |
|---|---|---|
| **Mobility x2 — POSITIVE CONTROL** | **2127** | **+23** ✅ the options are genuinely wired |
| baseline | 2104 | — |
| Passed Pawns off | 2070 | **−34** |
| Mobility off | 2047 | **−57** |
| Pawn Structure off | 2026 | **−78** |
| King Safety off (`Coefficient=1`) | 2022 | **−82** |

★ **The positive control is not optional and it earned its place**: had 0 and 200 both left the score
unmoved, the option would not be wired to what we assume and every row above would be void. +23 at x2 and
−57 at 0 is a clean two-sided response.

### WHAT IT SAYS
1. ★★ **PASSED PAWNS CARRIES THE LEAST — by a factor of ~2.4 vs king safety.** This answers the owner's
   question ("if SF beats us on passer positions, is it their passer stuff or other things better
   balanced?"): **other things.** It also retrospectively explains the 09-21 passer-ladder null — we spent
   a day on the subsystem that carries least even in a reference that beats us at equal nodes.
2. **King safety and pawn structure are the load-bearing pair** (−82, −78), mobility third (−57).
3. ⚠️ **Each number is an UPPER BOUND.** Zeroing one term leaves SF1.1 mis-tuned, so the drop includes the
   damage of being unbalanced, not just that term's marginal value. And the four are NOT additive
   (they sum to 251 against a total v2 gap of +250 at d10 / +137 at equal nodes -- a coincidence, not a
   decomposition).
4. ⚠️ It is **SF1.1's** balance, not SF11's and not ours. It ranks what matters in a hand-tuned HCE of our
   term set; it does not tell us our own constants are wrong in the same proportions.
5. ⚠️ One position emitted a python-chess PV-parse exception (`pv c4a6 h4d8 ...`) during the Pawn Structure
   arm; it did not register as a scoring failure, but a repeat run should watch for it.

⇒ **Feeds the retune spec** (`dev_notes/EVAL-V2-RETUNE-PLAN-2026-09-22.md`): the load-bearing subsystems
are the ones whose magnitudes most deserve room to move, and `PASSER_V2_MAG`'s wide range is now justified
as cheap insurance on a never-gamed knob rather than as a high-expectation bet.
