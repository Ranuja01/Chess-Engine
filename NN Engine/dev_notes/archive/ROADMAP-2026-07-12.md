## THE FRAME (measured, sharpened)
The collapse/over-push is a **broken refutation pipeline**, not an eval hole and not one bad knob. For an
over-push to be avoided, its refutation must survive 5 stages: **(1) generated → (2) ordered early enough →
(3) not reduced away → (4) its consequence visible in the leaf eval → (5) backed up to the root.** SF11 at
d6 has all five; **we have TWO broken: (2) ordering** (frozen move-gen cache freezes order at first
generation → a never-cut refutation stays buried across iterations; FMC ~87%) and **(4) leaf visibility**
(a shallow leaf doesn't register the refutation's consequence — loose pieces / king danger — so nothing
backs up; SF11's threats/KS make its d6 leaves say what our d12 leaves must COMPUTE).

**The AND-structure is why every single-lever knob was NEUTRAL** (singular = stage-5 polish; OTV = re-search
with the same blind ordering+leaves; extensions = deepen already-found lines; pruning-relaxed = un-reduce
moves still ordered last + mis-scored). A single-stage fix to a two-stage-broken pipeline is structurally
≈0. **We are at a local optimum of SINGLE-LEVER changes, not of the engine** ⇒ test stage-completing PAIRS.

Evidence: our over-push avoidance d6/10/14/18 = 36/42/51/68% vs SF11 66/83/94 (our d18 ≈ SF11 d6 = ~3×
selectivity gap; monotonic & accelerating). SF11-STATIC agrees with ours (both prefer the push, −95/−92cp)
⇒ the SEARCH corrects it, and it's CLASSICAL (SF11, not NNUE). Full: `collapse-search-efficiency-diagnosis-2026-07-12.md`.
